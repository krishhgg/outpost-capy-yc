## 1. Creator analytics

### Mechanics, step by step

YouTube Studio, the Realtime report.

1. The creator opens YouTube Studio. The overview shows a Realtime card. YouTube says it gives "early insights into the performance of your recently published videos," with top videos and subscriber count, filterable by videos or Shorts [primary] ([YouTube Studio Help](https://support.google.com/youtubecreatorstudio/answer/9314414?hl=en)).
2. Tapping the card opens an expanded report that compares "60 minute and 48 hour performance" [primary] (same page).
3. The same overview can show "comparisons to your typical performance," which explain why views are higher or lower than usual [primary] (same page). For retention, "typical" means the last 10 videos of similar length [primary] ([YouTube Help, content tab tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217)).
4. YouTube labels the numbers as estimates. "Realtime Analytics shows only estimates" and may not match the watch page count [primary] ([YouTube Help, how views are counted](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2991785?hl=en)).
5. Counts can lag, freeze or be adjusted. YouTube says metrics may take time to appear "during the first few hours," that it may slow, freeze or adjust counts to check accuracy, and that verified metrics can take several days [primary] (same page).
6. The definition of a view changed recently. From 24 August 2026, "views are counted the moment a video starts to play across all formats, including Shorts." Monetization still uses "engaged views" [primary] (same page).

The official refresh rate of the Realtime card is [not found]. Third-party guides disagree. One says every 10 seconds for the 25 latest uploads, others say every 1 to 3 minutes [3p] (search summaries; no official source).

YouTube milestones.

- In July 2021 YouTube tested "achievement cards" in the Studio dashboard for milestones in subscribers, views and watch hours. YouTube said the goal was to "better recognize creators and their amazing work." The article's example card marked 500 views [journalism] ([Social Media Today, 8 July 2021](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-adds-new-channel-achievement-cards-to-incentivize-creator-activity/603060/)).
- The physical Creator Awards start much higher: Silver at 100,000 subscribers, Gold at 1,000,000, Diamond at 10,000,000 [primary] ([YouTube Creators, awards](https://www.youtube.com/creators/how-things-work/programs-initiatives/awards/)).

TikTok and Instagram analytics. I could not open TikTok's analytics help page (it renders client-side) and could not search for Instagram's. Official refresh and delay rules for both are [not found].

Shopify's "cha-ching".

1. A sale comes in. The Shopify mobile app sends a push notification.
2. The default order sound is a cash register "cha-ching" [opinion] ([Shopify Community thread](https://community.shopify.com/t/you-have-a-sale-voice-now-on-push-notification/159308)).
3. In October 2022 Shopify added a voice saying "You've got sales." A Shopify staff member replied that it "will only play twice a lifetime before reverting back to the classic 'cha-ching' sound" [primary, Shopify staff in Shopify's official forum] (same thread, 14 October 2022).
4. Merchants asked for the cha-ching back. Quotes include "I hope they fix this so we can keep the kaching, as it is helpful" and "I miss the Ka-ching" [opinion] (same thread).
5. Shopify has no published history or research on the sound. Its origin and designer are [not found].

Stripe. The Stripe Dashboard iOS app says you can "Get instant notifications about new payments and customers" and opt into daily summaries [primary] ([App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/apple-store/id978516833)). Several paid third-party apps exist only to add a cha-ching sound to Stripe sales. One says "Every time you receive a payment... you'll hear a wonderful 'Cha-ching' sound" [3p] ([Charge for Stripe FAQ](https://chargeforstripe.com/faq/), [ChaChingly](https://chachingly.com/)). That people pay for this is a market signal that sale sounds matter to sellers. It is not evidence of an effect on behavior.

Substack. Writers get an email for each new free subscriber and each new paid one, with source and plan [opinion] ([Cloud Native Creator](https://cloudnativecreator.substack.com/p/substack-subscribers-automation-part-1)). [opinion] ([Gregory Gore](https://gregorygore.substack.com/p/new-milestone-thank-you)).

### Evidence on checking and well-being

How often creators check.

- Creators 4 Mental Health and Lupiani Insights surveyed more than 500 North American creators (reported November 2025). 65% said they obsess over content performance. 58% said their self-worth declines when content underperforms. Creators who check analytics on every post several times a day had significantly worse emotional well-being scores. The report recommends limiting checks to once a day or less [journalism reporting a 3p survey] ([Entrepreneur, 12 Nov 2025](https://www.entrepreneur.com/living/i-cant-afford-to-take-breaks-content-creators-are/499525)). The full report and the exact share of daily checkers are [not found]. This is a correlation. It does not show that checking causes lower well-being.
- Christin and Lewis interviewed YouTube "drama" creators. Metrics tracked both income and status for them, and "traffic numbers can turn into a spectacle of their own." Even so, creators used "distancing strategies" to keep metrics at arm's length [academic] ([Social Media + Society, 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2056305121999660)).
- A 2025 scoping review of content creators' health lists anxiety, exhaustion, burnout and pressure, mostly from small interview studies [academic] ([JMIR Public Health, 2025](https://publichealth.jmir.org/2025/1/e76708)).

Refresh behavior.

- Oulasvirta et al. describe a "checking habit," defined as brief, repeated inspection of dynamic content that is quick to reach on the phone. In a field experiment, checking emerged and was reinforced by informational "rewards" that were very quickly accessible [academic] ([Personal and Ubiquitous Computing, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00779-011-0412-2)). Press coverage of the study says "A typical checking lasts less than 30 seconds," and that users called it an annoyance, not an addiction [journalism] ([ScienceDaily, July 2011](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2011/07/110725101222.htm)).

What platforms learned from hiding numbers.

- YouTube hid public dislike counts after a 2021 test. YouTube said "our experiment data showed a reduction in dislike attacking behavior," and that this attacking "does occur at a higher proportion on smaller channels." Creators still see exact dislikes in Studio [primary] ([YouTube blog, 10 Nov 2021](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/update-to-youtube/)). YouTube published no effect sizes [not found].
- Instagram tested hidden like counts from 2019 "to see if it might depressurize people's experience." The result was "beneficial for some, and annoying to others." Instagram then made hiding optional for everyone [primary] ([Instagram, 26 May 2021](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/giving-people-more-control)). Adam Mosseri said it "didn't actually change nearly as much about the experience, in terms of how people felt or how much they use the experience, as we thought it would," and that it was "pretty polarizing." He said posting effects were "small effects, some positive, some negative" [journalism] ([TechCrunch, 26 May 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/05/26/facebook-and-instagram-will-now-allow-users-to-hide-like-counts-on-posts/)). Instagram never released the underlying data [not found].
- Ben Grosser argued the test was built to find little effect, because hiding was opt-in and every other metric stayed visible [opinion] ([Institute of Network Cultures, June 2021](https://networkcultures.org/blog/2021/06/14/ben-grosser-why-instagrams-like-hiding-saga-is-a-pr-stunt/)).

## 2. Money apps with live outcomes

### Mechanics, step by step (Robinhood, from the Massachusetts filings)

These come from the Massachusetts Securities Division's December 2020 administrative complaint, which I read via OCR [primary] ([complaint copy](http://brokeandbroker.com/PDF/RobinhoodComplaintMass.pdf); [state library record](https://archives.lib.state.ma.us/handle/2452/852254)), and the later consent order [primary] ([consent order PDF](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/securities/download/RH-Consent-Order.pdf)).

1. Sign-up lure. New customers could win free stock and had to mimic "scratching off" a lottery ticket to reveal it. The offer highlighted "Microsoft, Visa, or Apple" despite a low chance of getting those [primary] (consent order para. 31(vii)).
2. Referral loop. Existing customers could earn up to $500 a year in free stock by referring others [primary] (complaint para. 47).
3. First screen. A "First List" of stocks "chosen based on their popularity on Robinhood's platform" sat on the home screen. A "100 Most Popular" list was searchable [primary] (consent order para. 31(i)-(ii)).
4. Nudges. After funding, some customers got a one-time push: "Top Movers: Choosing stocks is hard. [flexing bicep emoji] Get started by checking which stock prices are changing the most." Others got a push linking to the 100 Most Popular list [primary] (consent order para. 31(iv)-(v)).
5. Daily value pushes. "Customers receive daily push notifications regarding the change in value of stocks in their accounts" [primary] (complaint para. 49).
6. Celebration. "Each time a customer makes a trade on Robinhood's platform, confetti rains down on the screen" [primary] (complaint para. 41). The consent order describes confetti after a customer's first trade [primary] (para. 31(vi)).
7. Daily return mechanic. For a cash-management waitlist, customers could improve their rank by tapping a picture of a debit card up to 1,000 times a day. At the cap they saw "out of taps today! Come back tomorrow if you're feeling tappy." People who did not tap daily watched their position fall [primary] (complaint paras. 43-45).

### Evidence

Engagement.

- Robinhood's S-1: "During 2020, among our customers who visited our app in a given day, they did so nearly seven times a day on average" [primary] ([S-1, 2021](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1783879/000162828021015656/robinhoodresales-1.htm)).
- The complaint says at least 670 customers with limited or no investment experience averaged at least five trades per day. One customer with no experience made 12,748 trades since 1 February 2020, about 92 per day [primary] (complaint intro and para. 56). About 68% of Massachusetts customers approved for options had limited or no experience [primary] (complaint).

What Robinhood stopped, and what the order bans.

- Robinhood stopped confetti on 31 March 2021 and the scratch-off reveal on 5 April 2021. It ended the waitlist tapping and the list-linked pushes by January 2022 [primary] (consent order para. 32).
- For Massachusetts customers the order requires Robinhood to "Remove all emojis from the life-cycle of a transaction," to stop confetti "or other celebratory imagery directly tied to frequency of trading," to stop "generalized push notifications highlighting specific lists," to never again use a waitlist tapping feature, and to "Permanently cease features that mimic games of chance" [primary] (consent order section C).

Does gamification change behavior?

- In a randomized online experiment, hedonic gamification (confetti, badges) raised trading volume by 5.17% on average. Most of the gap between gamified and plain platforms came from who chose them, 70% self-selection versus 30% from the gamification itself. People with lower financial literacy preferred the gamified platform. Price-trend notifications helped people with accurate beliefs and reinforced mistakes for people with wrong beliefs [academic] ([Chapkovski, Khapko and Zoican, Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2022.02650)).
- Robinhood users did more attention-driven trading than other retail investors, partly because of the app's features. Intense buying by Robinhood users predicted average 20-day abnormal returns of -4.7% for the top stocks bought each day [academic] ([Barber, Huang, Odean and Schwarz, Journal of Finance, 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.13183)).

Other regulators.

- FINRA fined Robinhood $57M plus nearly $13M in restitution in June 2021, citing "false or misleading information" given to millions of customers, options approvals and the March 2020 outages [journalism] ([CNBC, 30 June 2021](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/06/30/robinhood-to-pay-70-million-for-misleading-customers-and-outages-the-largest-finra-penalty-ever.html)).
- The SEC asked for public comment on "digital engagement practices" in August 2021, naming "behavioral prompts, differential marketing, game-like features (commonly referred to as gamification)" [primary] ([SEC release 2021-167](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2021-167)). In July 2023 it proposed rules on conflicts of interest from "predictive data analytics" [primary] ([SEC release 2023-140](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2023-140)). Whether that proposal was later withdrawn is not verified this session.

Acorns Round-Ups.

1. The user links a card.
2. Each purchase is rounded up to the next dollar. A $30.45 bill invests $0.55.
3. Spare change is held until it totals at least $5, then invested. Users can multiply Round-Ups by 2x, 3x or 10x [primary] ([Acorns Round-Ups page](https://www.acorns.com/round-ups/)).
4. Acorns says the average customer invests over $180 in the first four months with Round-Ups, and claims 16 million all-time customers [company-reported] (same page).

The design point for Outpost is the $5 threshold. Acorns bundles tiny amounts into one bigger, less frequent event.

Cash App and Coinbase. Cash App had 57 million monthly transacting accounts in 2023 [journalism] ([CNBC via Wikipedia](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/05/02/block-sq-earnings-q1-2024.html)). It has run a weekly "#SuperCashAppFriday" cash giveaway since 2017 [opinion] (cited on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_App)). Engagement data on Cash App's investing features or Coinbase price alerts: [not found].

Prediction markets (live outcome checking).

- Kalshi got a CFTC license in November 2020. Sports are reported as more than 90% of site activity (Financial Times) and 89% of 2025 revenue (Yahoo Finance). The WSJ reported 2.9 unprofitable Kalshi users for every profitable one. Several states have sued or blocked Kalshi's sports contracts in 2025 and 2026, including Massachusetts, Washington, Nevada and New York [journalism, via the Wikipedia summary; I did not open each source] ([Wikipedia: Kalshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalshi)).
- The CFTC fined Polymarket $1.4M in January 2022 for offering unregistered event contracts [primary] ([CFTC release 8478-22](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8478-22)). The WSJ reported that more than 70% of Polymarket users lose money and 0.1% of accounts take 67% of profits [journalism, via Wikipedia] ([Wikipedia: Polymarket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymarket)).
- Check frequency or session data for either app: [not found].

## 3. Fantasy sports and sports betting

### Mechanics, step by step

My description of the general pattern, not sourced to a single document:

1. Before the game, the user picks a lineup or places a bet. The app shows projected points or odds.
2. During the game, scores update live. Fantasy apps show projected versus actual points. Betting apps show live odds, cash-out offers and in-game bets.
3. The "sweat" is the period when the outcome is uncertain and the user watches it move. The game schedule fixes when it happens.
4. After the game, the result is final. A new slate opens.

In-play features documented in research: a 2018 scoping study found that 26% of 338 betting websites offered at least one in-play feature such as cash-out. It concluded in-play betting "has the potential to be more harmful" because of its structural features [academic] ([Killick and Griffiths, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11469-018-9896-6)).

### Evidence

- DraftKings had 3.7 million average monthly unique payers in 2024, up from 2.7 million in 2023. Average revenue per payer was $106 in 2024 and $113 in 2023 [primary] ([DraftKings 10-K for 2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1883685/000188368525000010/dkng-20241231.htm)). That is about 37% growth in payers (my arithmetic). Its risk factors tie engagement to "the availability of live sporting events" [primary] (same filing).
- ProPublica ran a DraftKings account for 10 weeks while betting like a problem gambler. It got a VIP invitation the day after losing nearly $1,800 in one night. Push alerts sometimes came six a day, one ending "Bet, settle, repeat!" Quick-deposit buttons rose from $10/$20/$50 to $500/$750/$950. A company official said only about 8% of active Massachusetts users used responsible-gaming tools. The article cites Connecticut data in which 1.8% of problem gamblers made up 51% of sports betting revenue [journalism] ([ProPublica, 26 Sept 2026](https://www.propublica.org/article/draftkings-sports-gambling-problem-vip-fanduel)).
- Lawsuits and regulators: Baltimore sued DraftKings and FanDuel in April 2025 over "predatory" practices. The Massachusetts Gaming Commission's penalties led DraftKings to stop accepting credit cards in 2023 [journalism, via Wikipedia] ([Wikipedia: DraftKings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DraftKings)).
- UK rules for online casino games, published February 2021. "The gambling system must not celebrate a return which is less than or equal to the total stake gambled," where celebrate means "auditory or visual effects that are associated with a win." Slots need at least 2.5 seconds between game cycles. Features that let the player "reduce the time until the result is presented," such as turbo or slam stop, are banned [primary] ([UK Gambling Commission RTS 14](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/standards/remote-gambling-and-software-technical-standards/rts-14-responsible-product-design)).
- ESPN or Yahoo fantasy check frequency on game days: [not found]. Share of US handle bet live: [not found]. Fantasy Sports and Gaming Association participation data is members-only [primary] ([FSGA](https://thefsga.org/industry-demographics/)).

## 4. Strava and fitness

### Mechanics, step by step

1. The user records a run or ride. The real effort is the input.
2. The activity posts to followers. Followers give "kudos," a one-tap approval.
3. Segments rank efforts on stretches of road or trail.
4. "Local Legend" goes to "the athlete who completes a given segment the most over a rolling 90-day period, regardless of pace or speed" [primary] ([Strava support](https://support.strava.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043099552-Local-Legends)). This lets slow but consistent people win something.
5. Once a year, "Year in Sport" sums up the user's activity.

### Evidence

- Strava reports 14 billion kudos given in its 2025 reporting year and over 180 million users [company-reported] ([Strava press, Dec 2025](https://press.strava.com/articles/strava-releases-12th-annual-year-in-sport-trend-report-2025)). BikeRadar reports this was a 20% rise from 2024, covering 1 September 2024 to 30 August 2025 [journalism] ([BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/strava-year-in-sport-2025)). That implies about 11.7 billion in 2024 (my arithmetic).
- Group activities get about twice the kudos of solo ones [company-reported] ([Strava mid-year 2025](https://press.strava.com/articles/strava-mid-year-data-shows-how-athletes-are-tracking-toward-2025-goals)).
- A study of 329 runners in five Dutch Strava clubs used longitudinal network models. It found that receiving kudos led runners to run more and more often [academic] ([Franken, Bekhuis and Tolsma, Social Networks, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socnet.2022.10.001)). Press coverage adds that athletes with kudos turned off ran less. In the pre-study survey, 83% used Strava to log activities, 78% to track progress, 31% for social contact and 21% for validation [journalism] ([Canadian Running](https://runningmagazine.ca/the-scene/new-study-says-strava-kudos-motivate-you-to-run-more/)). It is observational, not randomized.
- Across about 1.1 million runners over five years, exercise was socially contagious. The study used weather shocks to separate influence from selection [academic] ([Aral and Nicolaides, Nature Communications, 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms14753)).
- Strava retention numbers tied to kudos or segments: [not found].

## 5. Research on variable real-world rewards, and the ethics line

### Reward prediction and uncertainty

- Dopamine neurons appear to signal the gap between predicted and actual reward [academic] ([Schultz, Dayan and Montague, Science, 1997](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.275.5306.1593)).
- A separate dopamine response "covaried with uncertainty" and rose gradually until the expected time of reward. Uncertainty is maximal at a 50% chance [academic] ([Fiorillo, Tobler and Schultz, Science, 2003](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1077349)). Waiting for an uncertain outcome is itself part of the reward.
- People worked harder for an uncertain reward than for a better certain one. 70% finished a water-drinking task for a coin flip between $1 and $2, against 43% for a sure $2. A follow-up found 25% more water drunk. The effect appeared only when people focused on the process of pursuing the reward, not the outcome [academic] ([Shen, Fishbach and Hsee, Journal of Consumer Research, 2015](https://faculty.chicagobooth.edu/-/media/faculty/ayelet-fishbach/research/uncertainty_jcr.pdf)).

The implication for Outpost is that real outcomes already vary. The team does not need to add randomness to get the uncertainty effect.

### Social rewards

- Teens shown photos with many likes showed more activity in reward-related brain regions, and liked those photos more themselves [academic] ([Sherman et al., Psychological Science, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797616645673)).
- How strongly the nucleus accumbens responded to gains in one's own reputation predicted Facebook use. Its response to money did not [academic] ([Meshi, Morawetz and Heekeren, 2013](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2013.00439)).

### Notifications and checking

- A randomized field experiment (n = 237) compared normal notifications, batches three times a day, and none. The batched group felt more attentive, productive and in control, and reported less stress. The no-notification group got few of those benefits and reported more anxiety and fear of missing out [academic] ([Fitz et al., Computers in Human Behavior, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2019.07.016)).
- In a 24-hour test with 30 people, turning notifications off left people less distracted and more productive, but some felt anxious and less connected [academic] ([Pielot and Rello, MobileHCI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3098279.3098526)).
- The paper "Silence your phones" reports that smartphone notifications increase inattention and hyperactivity symptoms [academic] ([Kushlev, Proulx and Dunn, CHI 2016](https://doi.org/10.1145/2858036.2858359)). I could not open the full text to get the sample size.
- In a randomized experiment, the authors estimate that "self-control problems cause 31 percent of social media use," and that temporary incentives to cut use had lasting effects, consistent with habit formation [academic] ([Allcott, Gentzkow and Song, American Economic Review, 2022](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20210867)).

### Gambling features that regulators and researchers flag

- Losses disguised as wins. In a lab study of 40 novices, skin-conductance arousal was similar for real wins and for "wins" smaller than the stake, and both were higher than for plain losses [academic] ([Dixon et al., Addiction, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1360-0443.2010.03050.x)). The UK rule quoted in section 3 bans celebrating these [primary].
- Near misses. Near-miss slot outcomes felt less pleasant than full misses but raised the desire to keep playing, and activated win-related brain circuits. The effect held only when the player had a sense of control [academic] ([Clark et al., Neuron, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2008.12.031)).

### Dark patterns and regulation

- A crawl of about 11,000 shopping sites found 1,818 dark-pattern instances of 15 types in 7 categories. 183 sites used outright deceptive practices [academic] ([Mathur et al., CSCW 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.07032)).
- The FTC staff report "Bringing Dark Patterns to Light" (September 2022) describes "Sophisticated Dark Patterns Designed to Trick and Trap Consumers" [primary] ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light)).
- EU Digital Services Act Article 25 says online platforms "shall not design, organise or operate their online interfaces in a way that deceives or manipulates" users or impairs "free and informed decisions" [primary] ([DSA Art. 25 text](https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_25.html)).
- The European Parliament voted 545 to 12, with 61 abstentions, in December 2023 to ask for new rules against addictive design. It named "infinite scroll, default auto play, constant push and read receipt notifications" and asked for a list of good practices such as notifications off by default [primary] ([European Parliament, 12 Dec 2023](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20231208IPR15767/new-eu-rules-needed-to-make-digital-platforms-less-addictive)).
- Bhargava and Velasquez argue that designing social media to be addictive is "impermissible because it unjustifiably harms users in a way that is both demeaning and objectionably exploitative" [academic, normative] ([Business Ethics Quarterly](https://doi.org/10.1017/beq.2020.32)).

### Where the ethics line falls, as regulators have drawn it

Read together, the Massachusetts order, the UK rules and the DSA treat these as over the line:

- celebration tied to how often the user acts, not to what happened
- celebrating an outcome that is a loss
- reveals that copy games of chance (scratch-offs)
- penalties for not opening the app daily (the waitlist)
- generic nudges toward what is popular
- interfaces that mislead about money

They treat feedback on real outcomes, user choice and calm defaults as acceptable.

## 6. The small-numbers problem

### What the research says

- Early in a goal, pointing attention at progress already made motivates more than pointing at what is left ("20% completed is more impactful than 80% remaining"). Near the goal it flips. The authors say this works through "an illusion of fast progress" [academic] ([Koo and Fishbach, Journal of Consumer Research, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1086/663827)).
- Car wash loyalty cards needing 8 stamps were completed 19% of the time. Cards needing 10 stamps with 2 already given were completed 34% of the time [academic; numbers via secondary summaries, original at [Nunes and Drèze, JCR 2006](https://doi.org/10.1086/500480)]. That is about a 79% relative lift (my arithmetic). Secondary summaries say the effect disappeared when no reason was given for the head start [opinion] ([summary](https://productphilosophy.com/articles/goal-gradient-progress-mechanics)).
- Café customers bought coffee more often as they got closer to a free one. A 12-stamp card with 2 bonus stamps was finished faster than a plain 10-stamp card. A stronger speed-up near the goal predicted better retention [academic] ([Kivetz, Urminsky and Zheng, Journal of Marketing Research, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39)).

### What apps do

- They compare the creator to their own baseline. YouTube shows "comparisons to your typical performance" [primary] ([YouTube Studio Help](https://support.google.com/youtubecreatorstudio/answer/9314414?hl=en)).
- They put milestones low enough to reach. YouTube's 2021 achievement-card test covered small milestones (the reported example was 500 views), far below the 100,000-subscriber Silver award [journalism; primary for the award threshold] (sources above).
- They reward consistency over raw performance. Strava's Local Legend goes to whoever does a segment most often, "regardless of pace or speed" [primary] (source above).
- They batch tiny amounts. Acorns waits until spare change reaches $5 before investing [primary] (source above).
- They give firsts a different treatment. Shopify's "You've got sales" voice plays twice, then reverts to the standard sound [primary] (source above). Substack writers mark the first paid subscriber themselves [opinion].
- They let people hide numbers. Instagram made hiding likes optional [primary], and YouTube hid public dislikes partly because smaller channels were hit harder [primary].

## What carries over to Outpost

Each rule names the evidence behind it. Where a rule is my inference and not directly tested, it says so.

### Timing of the results reveal

1. Show results in fixed batches, not as a live ticker. Two or three refreshes a day is a reasonable start. Put the next update time on screen ("Next update 6:00 pm"). Evidence: batching three times a day beat both normal and zero notifications on well-being ([Fitz et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2019.07.016)) [academic]. Creators who checked several times a day had worse well-being ([C4MH via Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/living/i-cant-afford-to-take-breaks-content-creators-are/499525)) [journalism]. Applying batching to in-app results, not notifications, is my inference.
2. Because Outpost has no push, tell users when results will be ready. The no-notification group in Fitz et al. reported more anxiety and fear of missing out [academic]. A known release time is my proposed fix, not a tested one.
3. Make the newest results the first thing on open, and keep the check short. Quick access to new information is what forms a checking habit ([Oulasvirta et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00779-011-0412-2)) [academic]. That works for Outpost's daily-open goal. The same paper says users felt checking as an annoyance, so do not add steps or friction to reach the result.
4. Do not let pull-to-refresh change numbers between batches. A refresh that sometimes shows something new on demand is a variable-ratio loop. The UK rules move the other way, slowing play down (2.5 seconds minimum) and banning shortcuts that speed up results ([UKGC RTS 14](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/standards/remote-gambling-and-software-technical-standards/rts-14-responsible-product-design)) [primary].
5. Mark early numbers as estimates and show an "as of" time. YouTube's own Realtime figures are estimates and can freeze or change for days ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2991785?hl=en)) [primary].
6. Let the wait build toward a known reveal time. Dopamine activity related to uncertainty ramps up until the expected reward time ([Fiorillo et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1077349)) [academic]. Uncertain rewards motivate when people focus on the process ([Shen et al. 2015](https://faculty.chicagobooth.edu/-/media/faculty/ayelet-fishbach/research/uncertainty_jcr.pdf)) [academic]. Outpost's real outcomes are uncertain already, so do not add artificial randomness.

### Sound

7. Use a cash sound only when real money arrives, one sound per Whop sale. Never use it for views or likes. Evidence: Shopify's cha-ching is tied to orders, and merchants call it "helpful" and miss it when it is gone ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/you-have-a-sale-voice-now-on-push-notification/159308)) [opinion]. People also pay for third-party apps that add the sound to Stripe [3p]. No controlled study of sale sounds was found [not found].
8. Use a special sound for the first sale, play it a limited number of times, then switch to the standard sound. Shopify's "You've got sales" voice plays "twice a lifetime" before reverting [primary].
9. Make sound easy to turn off and follow the phone's silent switch. This is standard iOS practice. I could not open Apple's guidance this session, so verify it before building.

### Haptics

10. Use one success haptic for money events and nothing, or a light tick, for view updates. Keep the mapping fixed so the haptic means something. Evidence on haptics and engagement: [not found]. Treat this as a design default to test, not an evidence-backed rule.

### Animation and the base-builder layer

11. Animate outcomes, never the number of actions. Do not show confetti or a reward for "you swiped 20 videos" or "you posted 5 times." Celebrate a first sale, a views milestone, a first follower. Evidence: the Massachusetts order bans "celebratory imagery directly tied to frequency of trading" ([consent order](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/securities/download/RH-Consent-Order.pdf)) [primary]. Confetti and badges raised trading 5.17% and drew in less literate users ([Chapkovski et al.](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2022.02650)) [academic]. Outpost is not a broker. This is the nearest legal precedent.
12. The vault should fill with net money, or show gross and costs side by side. If a day's sales are below what the user paid for Outpost or for ads, do not play a win animation. Evidence: UK rules ban celebrating "a return which is less than or equal to the total stake" [primary]. Losses disguised as wins were as arousing as real wins ([Dixon et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1360-0443.2010.03050.x)) [academic].
13. The base must never decay because the user did not open the app. Only real outcomes move it. Evidence: Robinhood's waitlist dropped people who did not tap daily ("out of taps today! Come back tomorrow if you're feeling tappy"), and the order bans it permanently [primary].
14. No scratch-off, slot-reel or spin-to-reveal animation for money. A count-up from the last value to the new value is fine. Evidence: the order bans "features that mimic games of chance" after the scratch-off free-stock reveal [primary].
15. Keep the reveal a fixed length and skippable. The UK rules ban letting players speed up results because that increases play speed [primary]. For Outpost, the risk is the opposite: a long forced animation becomes a delay users learn to wait through. This rule is my inference.

### Framing small numbers

16. Early on, show what has been gained in total ("38 views across your first 3 videos"). Once a user is close to a milestone, show what is left ("12 views to 1,000"). Evidence: the small-area hypothesis ([Koo and Fishbach 2012](https://doi.org/10.1086/663827)) [academic]. Goal-gradient speed-up predicted retention ([Kivetz et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39)) [academic].
17. Compare each video to the user's own recent videos, not to other users. YouTube compares to "your typical performance" and to the last 10 similar videos [primary]. Christin and Lewis found metrics doubling as status, with creators distancing themselves from them [academic].
18. Add up results across TikTok, Shorts and Reels into one number per video, with a per-platform breakdown underneath. Acorns batches change until it reaches $5 [primary]. Adding platforms together is my inference. Label each platform's figure, since view definitions differ and YouTube changed its definition on 24 August 2026 [primary].
19. Start milestones low and space them roughly by doubling (for example 10, 50, 100, 500, 1,000 views; first follower; first click; first sale). YouTube's achievement cards included small milestones such as 500 views [journalism], while its formal awards start at 100,000 subscribers [primary]. The exact ladder should come from Outpost's own early data, because benchmarks were [not found].
20. Give consistency some credit, as Strava's Local Legend does ("regardless of pace or speed") [primary]. Keep it modest and avoid action-count celebrations (rule 11).
21. Do not fake a head start. Endowed progress works ([Nunes and Drèze 2006](https://doi.org/10.1086/500480)) [academic]. Summaries say it needs a stated reason [opinion]. Outpost can count real setup steps, such as the account connected, the first product created and the first video approved, as early progress. That is honest and fits the evidence.

### Celebrating firsts

22. Celebrate each first once, in full: first video live, first 100 views, first follower, first link click, first dollar. After that, smaller acknowledgments. Evidence: YouTube built achievement cards to "better recognize creators" [journalism]. Shopify limits its special voice to two plays [primary]. Substack writers celebrate their first paid subscriber on their own [opinion]. No controlled study of first-milestone celebrations was found [not found].
23. Let users share a first if they want to. Strava kudos and social feedback are tied to more activity ([Franken et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socnet.2022.10.001)) [academic]. Sharing is opt-in so Outpost's numbers stay private by default.

### User control

24. Offer a setting that hides counts and shows only trends or milestones. Instagram found hiding likes "beneficial for some, and annoying to others" and made it a choice [primary]. 58% of creators say their self-worth drops when content underperforms ([C4MH via Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/living/i-cant-afford-to-take-breaks-content-creators-are/499525)) [journalism].
25. When a number drops or a video flops, explain it in plain words: the platform adjusted counts, it is an estimate, or the video is under the user's typical. YouTube freezes and adjusts counts [primary]. FINRA cited "false or misleading information" shown to Robinhood customers [journalism].

### What to avoid, for ethical and legal reasons

- Celebration tied to how often the user swipes or posts. Massachusetts consent order [primary]. Chapkovski et al. [academic].
- Celebrating revenue when the user is net negative. UKGC RTS 14F [primary]. Dixon et al. [academic].
- "Almost viral" or "so close" copy on a single random result. Near misses raise the urge to keep going without any reward ([Clark et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2008.12.031)) [academic]. Showing distance to a cumulative milestone is different and fine (rule 16).
- Scratch-off, slot or spin reveals for money. Massachusetts consent order [primary].
- Streaks or ranks that decay when the user is away. Massachusetts complaint and order [primary].
- "Trending" or "popular" prompts that push users to post more of what others post. The order bans generic list-highlighting pushes [primary]. Robinhood herding predicted -4.7% returns ([Barber et al. 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.13183)) [academic]. For Outpost the harm would be lost money on cloned products, not stock losses. This parallel is my inference.
- Emojis and playful copy on money screens. The order requires removing emojis "from the life-cycle of a transaction" [primary]. That rule binds a broker, not Outpost, but it shows how regulators read playful money UX.
- More incentives for users who are losing. ProPublica documented VIP offers after heavy losses [journalism]. If Outpost ever sells boosts or credits, do not target users whose results are falling.
- Hard-to-reverse choices, repeated asks, and buried cancel paths. DSA Article 25 [primary]. FTC dark patterns report [primary]. Mathur et al. [academic].
- Showing other users' earnings to motivate. US earnings-claim rules likely apply to an app that promises income. I could not verify FTC earnings-claim guidance this session. Check it before any marketing or in-app social proof uses dollar figures.
