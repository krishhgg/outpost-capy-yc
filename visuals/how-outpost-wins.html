The four sponsors, on stage and under the hood

Whop

On stage

The product page appears after a swipe. A judge pays. The vault fills.

Under the hood

Products, prices, checkout links, the payment webhook, and marketplace search for raid targets. Search works with no key, in 0.8 s.

Convex

On stage

Everything on the phone updates live: feed, vault, stars, notebook.

Under the hood

Realtime queries, the scheduler, overnight crons, the webhook endpoint, video storage, and the job queue the Mac worker reads (1.1 s per video today).

Supermemory

On stage

The scout's notebook rewrites itself while you swipe, and the next cards change.

Under the hood

Every swipe and how long you looked. Agents search it before picking cards and before posting overnight.

Revyl

On stage

The close: "Revyl's AI played every build before it reached this phone." A grid of run videos, maybe one running live on the laptop.

Under the hood

YAML tests on cloud iPhone simulators, and proof on every pull request through Capy.

Capy is also required by the event rules. It writes the code and runs the Revyl proofs. Compound renders and posts the videos on the Mac and never comes up in the pitch.

What could still sink it

  • The base has to look like a game. It's the first thing judges see, and nothing about it is built or tested yet.
  • Wow 1 needs videos posted overnight. Real but small view counts are fine. Zero is not.
  • The Mac must stay awake with Compound open, or no new videos. Keep a stock of rendered videos as a fallback.
  • Whop, Supermemory and Revyl are untested until the keys arrive. The affiliate raid needs you to join programs by hand.
  • Nobody can prove retention in a one-day demo. The demo shows the loop, not D1 numbers.
