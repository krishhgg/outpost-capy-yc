Outpost UX: what the research says to build

Six deep-dive reports covering about 40 apps, distilled into screens. Full playbook in research/ux/00-playbook.md.

Five things the research changes

1
A daily drop you judge. Not an endless scroll.AI feeds made for watching lost their users: Sora kept under 8% at day 30, then shut down. The users who stayed came back to make and steer videos, about 70% of Sora return visits.
2
Every swipe visibly changes the next video.39.3% of YouTube control users felt nothing changed. Training on dislikes cut look-alikes 60.8%, against 22.7% when the dislike was only an input.
3
Results land at set times. Those times are the reason to open.No push. Coffee Meets Bagel's noon drop had 70% of users checking daily. Batching 3 times a day beat live delivery on stress (n=237).
4
Open on a living base. Show the work before the numbers.People judge a screen in 50 ms. Seeing the cooks raised rated food quality 22.2% in field tests.
5
Celebrate real outcomes only, sized to the outcome.Success-scaled effects raised enjoyment (n=1,699). Robinhood's confetti and scratch-off reveal were banned in Massachusetts.

One decision before building

You described doomscrolling your own videos. The research points to a finite drop instead. How should the feed work?

1
Daily drop of 8, plus "Make 4 more" on demand RecommendedFinite by default, and asking for more is a steering action, which is what brought Sora users back. Each extra batch shows your notes applied.
2
Endless doomscroll of your own videosClosest to your idea. Passive AI feeds churned, people grow 27% pickier across a session, and EU regulators are targeting infinite scroll.
3
Strict daily drop, Wordle styleStrongest scarcity, but no way to see the learning twice in one sitting, which the demo needs.
